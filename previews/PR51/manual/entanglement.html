Entanglement Measures ​

Every entropy-like quantity of a stabilizer state is a whole number of units, and the package returns that number as an Int: 1 means , so one ebit for qubits and one trit for qutrits. All functions on this page read only the X and Z rows, so they work with storephase=false, and they leave the represented state unchanged.

FunctionQuantity
entanglement_entropy(tab, A)von Neumann entropy
entanglement_profile(tab)  for every prefix l = 0, …, n
entanglement_negativity(tab, A, B)logarithmic negativity  
mutual_information(tab, A, B)    
tripartite_information(tab, A, B, C)  
conditional_mutual_information(tab, A, B, C)  

Subsystems are vectors of 1-based qudit indices. Every qudit outside the named subsystems is traced out.

Entropy ​

entanglement_entropy returns for pure and mixed states. For a pure state   with B the complement, and the function computes the rank on the smaller side. For a mixed state it is the entropy of the reduced state, which also counts the mixedness of the whole: the maximally mixed state has  .

For a stabilizer state the entropy is fixed by counting: with the stabilizer group and its elements supported on A,

which is why it is always a whole number.

julia
julia> ghz = StabilizerTableau(3, 4; state = :ghz);

julia> entanglement_entropy(ghz, [1]), entanglement_entropy(ghz, [1, 2])
(1, 1)

julia> entanglement_entropy(StabilizerTableau(3, 4), [1, 2])  # maximally mixed
2

entanglement_profile returns the entropies of all prefixes 1:l in one elimination, at the cost of a single entropy. An optional site order replaces the prefixes 1:l with order[1:l], for example to obtain the arcs of a periodic chain. entanglement_profile! writes into an existing vector without allocating.

julia
julia> entanglement_profile(ghz)
5-element Vector{Int64}:
 0
 1
 1
 1
 0

The destination of entanglement_profile! must be a writable, one-based Int vector of length n + 1 that does not share storage with the tableau; every entry is overwritten.

For a pure state a prefix and its complementary suffix have equal entropy, but the profile need not be symmetric: a Bell pair on sites 1 and 2 with an unentangled site 3 has profile [0, 1, 0, 0]. For a mixed state the last entry is n - m, the entropy of the whole state, and a maximally mixed chain has profile [0, 1, …, n].

With a site order, entry l + 1 is the entropy of order[1:l]. On a periodic chain a cyclic shift gives every arc that starts at a chosen site. Here two Bell pairs sit on the opposite bonds (1, 4) and (2, 3) of a ring of four:

julia
julia> ring = StabilizerTableau(3, 4; state = :product);

julia> for (a, b) in ((1, 4), (2, 3))
           apply!(ring, Fourier(a))
           apply!(ring, SUM(a, b))
       end

julia> entanglement_profile(ring) == [0, 1, 2, 1, 0]
true

julia> entanglement_profile(ring, [2, 3, 4, 1]) == [0, 1, 0, 1, 0]
true

The entropy of a mixed state does not separate entanglement from classical correlation. entanglement_negativity does: it is the logarithmic negativity of the reduced state of  ,

where is the partial transpose on B and   the trace norm, and it vanishes on classical correlations. For a pure state and B the complement of A it equals . In a three-qutrit GHZ state, any two qutrits share only a classical correlation once the third is traced out:

julia
julia> ghz = StabilizerTableau(3, 3; state = :ghz);

julia> entanglement_negativity(ghz, [1], [2]), mutual_information(ghz, [1], [2])
(0, 1)

julia> entanglement_negativity(ghz, [1]), entanglement_entropy(ghz, [1])
(1, 1)

Information measures ​

The three information measures are combinations of entropies:

mutual_information counts all correlations between A and B, quantum and classical. conditional_mutual_information is the correlation between A and B that C does not account for; it is nonnegative and zero exactly for a quantum Markov chain   . tripartite_information can take either sign: +1 for a GHZ state on three single qudits, negative for scrambled states. The three are related by        .

julia
julia> ghz = StabilizerTableau(3, 4; state = :ghz);

julia> mutual_information(ghz, [1], [2])
1

julia> conditional_mutual_information(ghz, [1], [2], [3])  # qudit 3 explains it
0

julia> tripartite_information(ghz, [1], [2], [3])
1

In the GHZ state the information shared by any two qudits is the same classical value, so it is counted twice and  , and conditioning on a third qudit leaves nothing. A scrambled state behaves in the opposite way: information about one part is spread over the others jointly. The four-qutrit state   , in which every pair of qutrits is maximally entangled with the other pair, is the extreme case. Qutrits 1 and 2 are uncorrelated on their own, but together with qutrit 3 they hold two units of correlation:

julia
julia> ame = StabilizerTableau(3, 4; state = :product);

julia> for g in (Fourier(1), Fourier(2), SUM(1, 3), SUM(2, 3), SUM(1, 4), SUM(2, 4, 2))
           apply!(ame, g)
       end

julia> mutual_information(ame, [1], [2]), mutual_information(ame, [1], [2, 3])
(0, 2)

julia> conditional_mutual_information(ame, [1], [2], [3])
2

julia> tripartite_information(ame, [1], [2], [3])
-2

With one subsystem, mutual_information takes the second to be its complement, which for a pure state gives twice the entanglement entropy. With C empty, conditional_mutual_information is the mutual information of A and B.

Cost and scratch space ​

Each function runs in at most time, cubic in n. The multi-entropy quantities share one elimination instead of calling entanglement_entropy repeatedly, which makes them cheaper on mixed states. All of them overwrite the tableau's scratch workspaces but never its generators, phases, destabilizers or canonicalization caches.

Subsystems are checked to hold distinct sites in 1:n, and the subsystems of one call to be disjoint, throwing ArgumentError otherwise. entanglement_entropy alone lets you skip that check with check=false; an invalid subsystem then gives a wrong result or an out-of-bounds access.
