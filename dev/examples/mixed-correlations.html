Correlations in mixed states ​

Entanglement entropy alone does not separate quantum from classical correlations once a state is mixed. This page compares the negativity with the mutual, tripartite and conditional mutual information.

Entanglement in mixed states: negativity ​

Mutual information S(A) + S(B) - S(AB), computed by mutual_information, counts classical correlations as well as entanglement. entanglement_negativity returns the logarithmic negativity log_d ‖ρ_ABᵀᴮ‖₁ of the state of A ∪ B with the other qudits traced out, which vanishes when the correlations are classical. A qutrit GHZ state shows the difference: two of its three qutrits share one unit of mutual information but no entanglement.

julia
julia> ghz = StabilizerTableau(3, 3; state=:ghz);

julia> mutual_information(ghz, [1], [2])
1

julia> entanglement_negativity(ghz, [1], [2])
0

julia> entanglement_negativity(ghz, [1], [2, 3])
1

With only one subsystem, the second is its complement. For a pure state the negativity then equals the entanglement entropy:

julia
julia> ghz = StabilizerTableau(3, 3; state=:ghz);

julia> entanglement_negativity(ghz, [1]) == entanglement_entropy(ghz, [1])
true

The negativity uses the same log-d units as the entropy. It takes at most cubic time in the number of sites, allocates nothing, works for both tableau types and with storephase=false, and leaves the generators unchanged.

Mutual and tripartite information ​

mutual_information(tab, A, B) returns S(A) + S(B) - S(AB), and tripartite_information(tab, A, B, C) returns

both in log-d units with every other qudit traced out. They equal the sums of entanglement_entropy calls but share the eliminations, which makes them about twice as fast on mixed states and as fast on pure ones.

The sign of separates two kinds of correlation. In a GHZ state, the information shared by any two qudits is the same classical value, so it is counted twice and  . In a scrambled state, information about one part is spread over the others jointly, and  . The four-qutrit state   , in which every pair of qutrits is maximally entangled with the other pair, is the extreme case:

julia
julia> ghz = StabilizerTableau(3, 4; state=:ghz);

julia> tripartite_information(ghz, [1], [2], [3])
1

julia> ame = StabilizerTableau(3, 4; state=:product);

julia> for g in (Fourier(1), Fourier(2), SUM(1, 3), SUM(2, 3), SUM(1, 4), SUM(2, 4, 2))
           apply!(ame, g)
       end

julia> mutual_information(ame, [1], [2]), mutual_information(ame, [1], [2, 3])
(0, 2)

julia> tripartite_information(ame, [1], [2], [3])
-2

With one subsystem, mutual_information takes the second to be its complement; for a pure state that gives twice the entanglement entropy. Like the negativity, both functions allocate nothing, leave the generators unchanged and work for both tableau types and with storephase=false.

Conditional mutual information ​

conditional_mutual_information(tab, A, B, C) returns

the correlation between A and B that C does not account for, again in log-d units with every other qudit traced out. It is never negative, and it vanishes exactly when    is a quantum Markov chain. Conditioning can remove correlation or create it. In the GHZ state, qudit 3 holds the same classical value that qudits 1 and 2 share, so given qudit 3 nothing is left:

julia
julia> ghz = StabilizerTableau(3, 4; state=:ghz);

julia> mutual_information(ghz, [1], [2]), conditional_mutual_information(ghz, [1], [2], [3])
(1, 0)

In the absolutely maximally entangled state above, qudits 1 and 2 are uncorrelated on their own, but together with qudit 3 they hold two units of correlation. The difference is the tripartite information,       :

julia
julia> ame = StabilizerTableau(3, 4; state=:product);

julia> for g in (Fourier(1), Fourier(2), SUM(1, 3), SUM(2, 3), SUM(1, 4), SUM(2, 4, 2))
           apply!(ame, g)
       end

julia> mutual_information(ame, [1], [2]), conditional_mutual_information(ame, [1], [2], [3])
(0, 2)

julia> tripartite_information(ame, [1], [2], [3])
-2

The result equals four entanglement_entropy calls but needs only one elimination, which makes it several times faster on mixed states. With C empty it is the mutual information of A and B.
