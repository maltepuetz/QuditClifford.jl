Measurements against boundary dephasing ​

Weinstein, Bao and Altman study a monitored circuit coupled to an environment at its two ends: Z. Weinstein, Y. Bao, E. Altman, Measurement-Induced Power-Law Negativity in an Open Monitored Quantum Circuit, Phys. Rev. Lett. 129, 080501 (2022), arXiv:2202.12905. An open chain of L qudits starts in . Every time step applies one layer of a brick wall of random two-qudit Clifford gates, measures each qudit in the Z basis with probability p and records the outcome, and then dephases the two end qudits completely in the Z basis,

which is dephase!. The dephasing acts as a bath that the chain keeps leaking information into, so the state stays mixed, and the measurements compete with it.

The circuit, the initial state, the Z measurements and the dephased end sites follow the paper, as does the order within a step that its supplement gives: gates, then measurements, then dephasing. The paper's numerics are for qubits. Its supplement dephases on even time steps only, while its first figure dephases between all layers; for the even L used here the two agree exactly, because the odd-step gates are the only ones that touch the end sites and the Z measurements commute with the dephasing. The paper evolves for time steps without a prefactor; the depth of 4L steps is our choice.

The first function runs one trajectory and returns the logarithmic negativity and the mutual information between the left and right halves of the chain, the two quantities the paper reports for this geometry. Phases do not affect either quantity, so the tableau stores none. The gates are preallocated, one per bond, and refilled with fresh random Cliffords as the circuit runs.

julia
using QuditClifford
using Random
using Statistics

# Run one trajectory of the boundary-dephased circuit on L qudits of dimension d.
# p is the measurement probability per site and step, depth the number of
# brick layers, and gates[i] the preallocated Clifford on the bond (i, i + 1).
function boundary_dephasing_trajectory(rng, d, L, gates; p, depth=4L)
    tab = DestabilizerTableau(d, L; state=:Z, storephase=false)

    for step in 1:depth
        # Odd steps act on the bonds (1,2), (3,4), …; even steps on (2,3), (4,5), ….
        for i in (isodd(step) ? 1 : 2):2:(L - 1)
            apply!(tab, random_clifford!(rng, gates[i]))
        end

        # Measure Z on each site with probability p, drawing the outcome from rng.
        for i in 1:L
            if rand(rng) < p
                measure!(rng, tab, SinglePauli(i, 0, 1))
            end
        end

        # Dephase both end sites in the Z basis; nothing is recorded.
        dephase!(tab, SinglePauli(1, 0, 1))
        dephase!(tab, SinglePauli(L, 0, 1))
    end

    left_half = 1:(L ÷ 2)
    return entanglement_negativity(tab, left_half), mutual_information(tab, left_half)
end

The next helper averages both quantities over trajectories and returns their means with standard errors. Both functions take the random number generator as an argument, so one seed reproduces the whole figure.

julia
# Average the half-chain negativity and mutual information over trajectories.
function boundary_dephasing_averages(rng, d, L; p, depth=4L, trajectories=200)
    # These draws only allocate the per-bond operators; every gate is refilled
    # before it is applied.
    gates = [random_clifford(rng, d, [i, i + 1]) for i in 1:(L - 1)]
    negativity = zeros(trajectories)
    information = zeros(trajectories)

    for t in 1:trajectories
        negativity[t], information[t] = boundary_dephasing_trajectory(
            rng, d, L, gates; p=p, depth=depth
        )
    end

    standard_error(x) = std(x) / sqrt(length(x))
    return (
        mean(negativity), standard_error(negativity),
        mean(information), standard_error(information),
    )
end

In every trajectory both quantities are whole numbers in log-d units: bits for qubits, trits for qutrits. These sizes are too small for the paper's fitted coefficients; At the paper's sizes compares with them. The plot shows qubits on the left and qutrits, which the paper does not simulate, on the right, for no measurements, for p = 0.1, below the qubit transition at  , and for p = 0.3, above it. Every point averages 200 trajectories, and the error bars are one standard error.

julia
using CairoMakie


rng = Xoshiro(2022)
dimensions = (2, 3)
probabilities = (0.0, 0.1, 0.3)

# results[(d, p)] holds one (E, ΔE, I, ΔI) tuple per chain length.
results = Dict(
    (d, p) => [
        boundary_dephasing_averages(rng, d, L; p=p, trajectories=trajectories)
        for L in chain_lengths
    ] for d in dimensions for p in probabilities
)

Without measurements both quantities vanish in every trajectory. The gates carry each stabilizer generator to the ends, where the dephasing removes it, nothing adds generators back, and after 4L steps the chain is maximally mixed. At p = 0.1 the measurements keep restoring information, and both quantities grow with L for qubits and qutrits alike. At p = 0.3 the qubit curves change little beyond L = 16: from L = 16 to L = 32 the negativity gains about a tenth of a bit, against about seven tenths at p = 0.1. That is consistent with the area law the paper finds above , although at these sizes slow growth cannot be told apart from saturation. The qutrit curves at p = 0.3 rise more slowly beyond L = 24, which suggests that the qutrit transition lies at a larger p, which the next section confirms. The mutual information, which also counts classical correlations, exceeds the negativity wherever either is nonzero, and it shows the same three regimes.

At the paper's sizes ​

The paper fits the power law over L = 40 to 280 with 200 realizations. Running that during every documentation build would take too long, so the averages below were computed once by docs/data/boundary-dephasing/reproduce.jl and stored next to it as JLD2 files; the build only reads and plots them. The script runs the same circuit as above, threaded over trajectories, for L = 40, 80, …, 280, 200 trajectories per point and depth 4L, with p in steps of 0.025 up to 0.3 for qubits and 0.5 for qutrits. One dimension up to p = 0.3 takes about 17 minutes on 12 threads of an Apple M3 Pro:

bash
julia --project=docs -t auto docs/data/boundary-dephasing/reproduce.jl 2
julia --project=docs -t auto docs/data/boundary-dephasing/reproduce.jl 3 0.5
julia
using JLD2

# Read the stored averages into stored[d][(L, p)] = (E, ΔE, I, ΔI).
function read_stored(d)
    path = joinpath(pkgdir(QuditClifford), "docs", "data", "boundary-dephasing",
        "results_d$(d).jld2")
    data = JLD2.load(path)
    return Dict(
        (L, p) => (data["E"][i, j], data["dE"][i, j], data["I"][i, j], data["dI"][i, j])
        for (i, p) in enumerate(data["p"]) for (j, L) in enumerate(data["L"])
    )
end

stored = Dict(d => read_stored(d) for d in dimensions)
paper_lengths = 40:40:280
# Stop short of the colormap's pale end so the largest L stays visible on white.
length_colors = [cgrad(:batlow)[x] for x in range(0, 0.8; length=length(paper_lengths))]

figure = Figure(size=(720, 420), figure_padding=9)
panels = [
    Axis(
        figure[row, column];
        title=row == 1 ? titles[column] : "",

For qubits the negativity is zero at p = 0, grows with L up to  , and the curves for all sizes meet between p = 0.175 and 0.2, past which the negativity no longer depends on L: the area law. That matches the paper's   within the resolution of this grid; locating it precisely needs a finite-size scaling collapse. The qutrit curves keep growing with L up to p = 0.3 and meet between p = 0.325 and 0.35, so the qutrit transition sits at roughly twice the qubit measurement rate. The mutual information follows the negativity at about twice its value.

At p = 0.1 the paper fits   . A weighted least-squares fit of the stored averages gives:

julia
# Fit y = c1 L^(1/3) + c2 with weights 1/σ²; return the coefficients and
# their standard errors.
function fit_cube_root(L, y, σ)
    X = hcat(L .^ (1 / 3), ones(length(L)))
    W = 1 ./ σ .^ 2
    A = X' * (W .* X)
    c = A \ (X' * (W .* y))
    covariance = inv(A)
    return c, sqrt.([covariance[1, 1], covariance[2, 2]])
end

fits = Dict(
    d => fit_cube_root(
        collect(paper_lengths),
        [stored[d][(L, 0.1)][1] for L in paper_lengths],
        [stored[d][(L, 0.1)][2] for L in paper_lengths],
    ) for d in dimensions
)

for d in dimensions
    (c1, c2), (σ1, σ2) = fits[d]

        ", c2 = ", round(c2; digits=3), " ± ", round(σ2; digits=3))
end
